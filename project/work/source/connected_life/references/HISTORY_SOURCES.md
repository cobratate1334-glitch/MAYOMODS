# Historical references and explicit game rules

Photographs have actual retrieval/source/license/hash records in
[photos.json](photos.json) and [photo credits](../inputs/photos/ATTRIBUTION.md).
The headlines summarize widely documented dates; archival pictures may illustrate
the broader era. Unit losses and training outcomes are fictional game results.

Background references for review:

- [UK Parliament — conscription](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/private-lives/yourcountryneedsyou/overview/conscription/)
- [US National Archives — WWII military records](https://www.archives.gov/research/military/ww2)
- [Canadian War Museum — conscription, 1917](https://www.warmuseum.ca/firstworldwar/history/life-at-home-during-the-war/recruitment-and-conscription/conscription-1917/)
- [US Office of the Historian — Cuban Missile Crisis](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/cuban-missile-crisis)
- [9/11 Memorial — September 11 primer](https://www.911memorial.org/learn/resources/911-primer)
- [VA — current GI Bill eligibility](https://www.va.gov/education/eligibility/)

The implemented cohorts are listed exactly in cohorts.py. They simplify changing
ages, exemptions, citizenship, overseas-service restrictions and split authorities.
They are not automatically derived from a legal document. The owner's requirement
of four completed honorable US service years is deliberately a game rule; it does
not replace the actual historical/current eligibility rules in the VA reference.
Canada/Australia's home/regional obligations and France/Italy's divided service
are explained instead of silently imposing one universal overseas draft.
